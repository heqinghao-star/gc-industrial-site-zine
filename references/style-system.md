# Style System

## Identity

**Deconstructivist industrial site-analysis travel zine**: a flat scanned paper field where a real place or structure is dismantled into measured photographic fragments and technical drawings that explain how the subject is organized.

## Fixed system

- **Canvas:** landscape 4:3 by default.
- **Surface:** warm drafting, survey, tracing, or archival paper with fibers, graphite dust, fold scars, xerox grain, and matte scan reproduction.
- **Attention geometry:** roughly 55–75% open paper; one related analytical cluster uses roughly 20–40%.
- **Subject treatment:** recognizable photo evidence combined with elevations, sections, axes, dimensions, hatches, or exploded details derived from the same subject.
- **Deconstruction:** fragments must express one structural thesis. They are not decorative cards.
- **Depth:** orthographic and printed; no glossy mockup depth or cinematic scene lighting.
- **Typography:** sparse monospaced or technical lettering, usually English uppercase, used as labels rather than advertising copy.
- **Color:** near-monochrome photo reproduction plus one source-derived accent occupying roughly 1–3% of the canvas.
- **Mood:** analytical, archival, spatial, rigorous, exploratory, and quietly poetic.

## Deconstruction grammars

Choose one primary grammar and optionally one subordinate move:

- **Exploded assembly:** unequal subject fragments displaced along a real load, circulation, or slope direction.
- **Folded section:** an image plane such as water, glass, shadow, or ground becomes a section datum and folds into technical drawing.
- **Interrupted elevation:** a long photographic elevation is broken by measured gaps that drawing lines bridge.
- **Radial mechanism:** a circular subject becomes an axis, radius, sector, and linked datum system.
- **Displaced detail:** one small structural module is extracted and connected back through a dimension or inference line.
- **Sectional wedge:** one narrow colored or hatched incision reveals path, access, load, light, or geological structure.

Do not select a grammar independently of the photographed subject.

## Palette extraction

1. Identify three to five visible source colors by role: dominant field, material, shadow, light, vegetation, water, or manufactured paint.
2. Reduce the photo fragments to charcoal, sepia, graphite, chalk, or low-saturation versions of those colors.
3. Select one accent from a meaningful source relationship:
   - manufactured safety paint for load or access;
   - water or glass color for reflection or datum;
   - sunset or rust for light and rotation;
   - vegetation or mineral color for terrain and ecology.
4. Convert the source color into a printable ink hue with enough contrast at thumbnail size.
5. Apply it only to the structural carrier: section hatch, axis, incision, module, small swatch, or focal datum.

Never default to Klein blue, cobalt, or any hue merely because a style reference used it. Reference accents are sample residue unless the target independently supports the same hue.

## Typography

- Prefer a title of two or three words and no more than three supporting labels.
- Labels should name visible analytical roles: `LOAD PATH`, `WATER DATUM`, `TREE BELT`, `WHEEL AXIS`.
- Do not invent precise numeric measurements unless supplied by the user or available from trustworthy data.
- Avoid decorative coordinates, fake archive codes, and meaningless engineering language.

## Sample residue

The following never becomes a reusable rule from a reference image:

- its exact blue stair, swatch, fragment positions, dimension angles, wording, subject, user interface, creator identity, brand, watermark, date, or location;
- a color that is not independently justified by the new target;
- a two-panel arrangement that happens to appear in one sample.

## Anti-identity

Avoid vivid full-color photographs, postcards, tourism campaigns, map graphics, mood-board cards, regular editorial grids, dense scrapbook layers, random arrows, decorative technical jargon, multicolor accents, glossy paper, hard shadows, dramatic perspective, 3D visualization, neon, cyberpunk, and generic architectural-diagram templates.
